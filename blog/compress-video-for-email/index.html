How to compress a video for email

By TinyWow Now team · Published 23 November 2026 · Updated 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

To compress a video for email, check your limit first. Gmail allows up to 25 MB of attachments for personal accounts. Then lower the resolution and raise the compression until the file fits with room to spare. Compress Video on TinyWow Now does this in your browser, so your video never uploads.

Know your limit before you compress

Gmail lets you attach up to 25 MB in total on a personal account. When you attach more, Gmail adds a Google Drive link instead. Work and school accounts differ, because the Google Workspace administrator sets their limits. Other providers set their own limits, so check yours before you start.

Email adds overhead to every attachment, so the message grows larger than the file. As a rule of thumb, aim for 15 to 18 MB when the limit is 25 MB. This margin is our own advice, not a figure from Google.

Work out the size your video needs

A video file grows with its bitrate and its length. Multiply the size you want in megabytes by 8, then divide by the length in seconds. The answer is the average bitrate, in megabits per second, that fits.

Video lengthTarget sizeAverage bitrate that fits
1 minute (60 s)18 MB2.4 Mbps (18 × 8 ÷ 60)
3 minutes (180 s)18 MB0.8 Mbps (18 × 8 ÷ 180)
5 minutes (300 s)18 MB0.48 Mbps (18 × 8 ÷ 300)

Compress Video applies this maths for you when you type a target size. The table shows why a longer video needs a lower bitrate, which means a smaller picture or stronger compression.

How to compress a video for email

  1. Open Compress Video and add your file.
  2. Type your size in Target size (MB), such as 18, or tap a preset. The tool then picks the bitrate for you.
  3. Set Resolution to Max 720p or Max 480p if you want to cap the picture size. Small targets lower the resolution on their own.
  4. Click Compress video, then read the sizes before and after.
  5. Download the MP4 and check that it plays. If the tool marks it as missing the target, follow the next section.

The tool compresses with H.264 video and AAC audio, and it never scales a video up. With a target size, it works out the bitrate from your video length. It then adjusts the bitrate over up to three passes and keeps the largest result that fits. It counts 1 MB as 1,000,000 bytes, so the file also fits limits that count the other way. Without a target, Balanced uses H.264 CRF 28 and Strong uses CRF 32. A higher number gives a smaller file with more visible loss.

We tested target sizes on 23 November 2026 with short test clips. A 0.5 MB target gave a 0.49 MB file, which is 98 percent of the target. A 0.25 MB target gave 89 percent, and the tool lowered the picture size to reach it. A clip that was already smaller than the target stayed unchanged. An extreme target of 0.12 MB for a five-second clip stayed out of reach, and the tool said so. Results on real footage vary, so always check the size.

What shrinks a video the most

  • A shorter video. Every second you cut removes data. Use Trim Video to keep only the part you need.
  • A lower resolution. A 1280 × 720 picture holds 921,600 pixels, against 2,073,600 at 1920 × 1080. That is more than half fewer pixels to store. Resize Video does only this step.
  • Stronger compression. Move from Balanced to Strong when the picture still looks good at the smaller size.

A ZIP file does not help. Video already uses compression, so a ZIP barely shrinks it and only bundles files.

When compression is not enough

Share a link instead. Gmail turns large attachments into Google Drive links, and you can upload the file to any cloud storage and send its link. The tool refuses files over about 700 MB, so trim a long recording first or share it as a link.

Sending the video on WhatsApp instead? Its limits differ by platform and change over time, so read the current WhatsApp help page for your device before you compress.

Does the video upload?

No. Compress Video runs on your device, in your browser. The first run downloads a video engine of about 30 MB, and your browser keeps it for next time. You can check this yourself with the Network tab, as our guide to merging PDF files without uploading them explains.

We ran this check on 23 November 2026 with a short test clip. The tool made four requests, all downloads of its own video engine files, and no request that sent data out. The compression finished in five seconds on a four-second clip, and longer videos take longer.

Frequently asked questions

What size should a video be for email?

It must fit your provider limit with room to spare. Gmail allows 25 MB in total for personal accounts, and Workspace administrators set their own limits for work and school accounts.

How do I send a video that is too big for email?

Compress it with Compress Video, or share a link. Gmail adds a Google Drive link automatically when your attachments exceed 25 MB.

Does compressing a video reduce its quality?

Yes, a little. The tool uses lossy H.264 compression, and stronger levels and lower resolutions show more loss. Keep your original file.

Which format should I send?

Send an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio, because it plays on nearly every device. Compress Video saves an MP4.

Can I set a target size such as 20 MB?

Yes. Type it in Target size (MB). The tool aims for it with up to three passes and keeps the largest result that fits. It does not promise an exact size, so check the result.

Does the tool upload my video?

No. The tool runs in your browser, on your device. You can confirm this in the Network tab of your developer tools.
